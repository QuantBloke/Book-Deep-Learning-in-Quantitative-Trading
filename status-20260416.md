## Part 2 — Trading / quant applications per notebook

Framing: for each notebook I list (a) what it is, (b) a concrete way to use the code or idea in live trading / research, (c) the obvious extension beyond the toy demo. The code in `Utilis/` (Sharpe loss, early-stopper, dataset wrapper, metrics) is reusable across all of these.

### Chapter 2 — Econometric foundations

**`01_statistics.ipynb` — return-distribution diagnostics (ADF, Jarque-Bera, ACF/PACF, skew/kurt on SPY)**

- **Use as a pre-trade sanity gate.** Wrap the ADF / JB / ACF-PACF calls into a `pretrade_checks(series)` function you run before any new signal goes live. Any of these four failing (non-stationary series, non-normal tails when your risk model assumes normality, autocorrelated residuals, kurtosis > 10) should block or flag the strategy.
- **Research application.** Run the same diagnostics on your **strategy-residual** series (strategy returns minus a benchmark regressed fit). A well-specified alpha should leave white-noise residuals — persistent ACF there means you are leaving signal on the table.
- **Extension.** Replace SPY with your book's top-20 instruments, cache results daily into a small SQLite/Parquet table, and chart drift over time — a spike in kurtosis or a flip from stationary to non-stationary is often the first visible sign of a regime shift.

**`02_time_series_models.ipynb` — AR/MA/ARMA fitting and forecasting**

- **Use as a benchmark any ML model must beat.** Before you deploy an LSTM/transformer for return forecasting, fit an ARMA(p,q) via the same statsmodels call. If your deep model's OOS error is within one s.e. of the ARMA baseline, you have overfit — ship the ARMA.
- **Order-book auto-quoting.** For market-making quote-skew dynamics or inventory drift, low-order ARMA on 1-second mid-price increments is often genuinely good and trains in milliseconds. The notebook's `ARIMA(order=(p,0,q))` + `.forecast()` pattern is exactly what you need.
- **Extension.** Drop in `statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax` to add exogenous regressors (VIX, yield-curve slope, cross-sectional momentum factor) — same API, richer signal.

**`03_volatility_clustering.ipynb` — ARCH(1) and GARCH(1,1) via the `arch` package**

- **Vol-targeted position sizing.** This is the building block the Chapter 5 TSMOM notebook already uses heuristically — `position = σ_target / σ_forecast`. Replace the rolling std with GARCH(1,1) one-step-ahead `σ_{t+1}` for a smoother and more responsive target, especially after a shock.
- **Options RV/IV spread.** Use GARCH 1-step and 10-step forecasts as your **realized-vol forecast**, compare against ATM implied vol, and trade the premium. The notebook's `forecast(horizon=10)` call gives you the term-structure of forecast variance directly.
- **Extension.** Swap Normal for a Student-t distribution (`arch_model(..., dist='t')`) — fat-tailed returns are the rule, not the exception, and the book simplifies this away.

### Chapter 3 — Regression, classification, and architectures

**`01_regression_classification.ipynb` — loss-function and metric catalogue**

- **Pick the right loss for the question.** The useful takeaway is the menu: MSE for return magnitude, Huber when outliers are noise (most of the time in finance), MAE when you want a median not a mean, F1 / confusion matrix for directional models. I would turn this into a `loss_for_task()` helper that my strategy templates call by name.
- **Extension.** Add **asymmetric / quantile loss** (pinball loss) to the menu. In trading, over- vs under-predicting a return has very different P&L consequences; quantile regression lets you target, e.g., the 30th percentile of next-day returns for stop-loss sizing.

**`02_regression_training.ipynb` — MLP / CNN / WaveNet / LSTM / Transformer side-by-side on a toy sequence problem**

- **Architecture shootout template.** This is the single most reusable notebook in the book: identical data loader, identical training loop, five architectures, early stopping. Adapt it to your real input — e.g. (`lookback`, `n_features`) = (60, 20 features including log-return, RSI, volume, spread, …) across 20 instruments — and you have a ready-made architecture bake-off.
- **WaveNet for intraday forecasting.** Dilated 1D convs are a sweet-spot for minute-bar features: they see a long history at low parameter count, which matters when you have years of minute data but limited distinct market regimes. The exact `WaveNet` class in the notebook ports directly.
- **Extension.** Replace the synthetic target with **next-bar volatility** rather than next-bar return — volatility is far more predictable, and volatility forecasts feed straight into sizing.

### Chapter 4 — ML workflow (Optuna + rolling CV)

**`01_workflow.ipynb` — Optuna HPO with rolling/forward cross-validation**

- **Use this exact harness for any new signal you research.** The rolling-forward CV loop at fractions [0.7, 0.8, 0.9] is the right protocol in finance — k-fold with shuffling leaks future information and is how people accidentally publish 2+ Sharpes. Replace the simulated `X, y` with your real dataset and you have a defensible research pipeline.
- **Production extension.** Wire the `optuna.study` into a persistent backend (`storage='sqlite:///studies.db'`) so every HPO run is logged, comparable, and restart-safe. Turns ad-hoc notebook tuning into a research log.
- **Extension.** Add a **purged & embargoed** walk-forward split (de Prado's Ch. 7 style) so labels whose feature windows overlap the test fold get dropped. This is the right fix for the label-leakage problem at sub-daily frequencies.

### Chapter 5 — Momentum

**`01_time_series_momentum.ipynb` — long-only, vol-targeted, TSMOM, SMA, MACD on AAPL**

- **Volatility-targeted wrapper around any strategy.** The single most valuable snippet is `position_t = σ_target / σ_realized_t` — drop it around every existing strategy you run. The notebook reports Sharpe jumping 0.58 → 0.85 just from vol targeting a long-only AAPL, which is consistent with the live trading reality: most of the Sharpe lift in simple strategies comes from risk sizing, not signal.
- **Cheap regime filter.** Use MACD sign or SMA(10) > SMA(40) as an on/off gate for a separate alpha you already trust — ride it only when the trend backdrop agrees.
- **Extension.** Replace AAPL with a futures basket (ES, ZN, CL, GC, 6E). Single-stock TSMOM is noisy; classic TSMOM literature is on futures for a reason.

**`02_cross_sectional_momentum.ipynb` — rank-decile long-short on a 10-stock panel**

- **Core sector-neutral equity strategy template.** The code is structurally complete: pivot wide, rank by 252d past return, long top decile / short bottom decile, daily rebalance. To make it a real factor, just (a) widen the panel to your 200–500 stock universe, (b) add sector neutrality (`groupby(sector).rank()`), and (c) add a transaction-cost model.
- **Why it scored Sharpe ≈0.07 here and why that's fine.** 10 stocks is below the diversification threshold where cross-sectional momentum works. Treat the low Sharpe as a feature of the toy setup, not evidence against the strategy family.
- **Extension.** Blend with **short-term reversal** (5-day return, short winners / long losers) — classic two-factor combination, and the same `groupby(date).rank()` scaffolding.

**`03_deep_momentum_strategy.ipynb` — MLP trained directly on Sharpe loss**

- **The most important idea in the book for applied quant: train the network on the utility you actually care about.** `Utilis/loss.py`'s `SharpeLoss` (neg mean / std of `weight * return`) lets you gradient-descend on Sharpe, turnover-adjusted Sharpe, or Sortino directly — no need to forecast returns as an intermediate step. This changes how you build signals.
- **Near-deployable path.** Take the exact MLP + SharpeLoss, feed a panel of 50+ assets × 30-day windows × 8 features, swap the output `Softsign` for a `Tanh` position in [-1, 1] per asset, add a turnover penalty `+ λ * mean(|w_t - w_{t-1}|)` to the loss, retrain. That is a non-trivial production-lite strategy.
- **Extension.** Replace the MLP core with the WaveNet or LSTM from Chapter 3 while keeping the Sharpe loss — the book does this conceptually; you can do it in a few dozen lines.

### Chapter 6 — Portfolio optimisation

**`01_classical_portfolio_optimization.ipynb` — min-variance, max-diversification, shrunk covariance**

- **Use `ShrunkCovariance` everywhere you currently use `pd.DataFrame.cov()`.** Sample covariance on 20+ assets with a 60-day window is an ill-conditioned disaster; Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage (or sklearn's equivalent) is a one-line fix that materially improves out-of-sample Sharpe. The notebook already shows min-variance Sharpe of 0.92 on the portfolio panel — that is mostly the shrinkage doing the work.
- **Pair with the deep allocator as an ensemble.** Run classical min-variance and the deep Sharpe-loss allocator side-by-side; blend weights 50/50 (or learn the blend). Diversifies model risk against each other.
- **Extension.** Add a **turnover constraint** — solve with `cvxpy` subject to `|w_t - w_{t-1}| <= τ` — or a factor-neutrality constraint (no net exposure to beta / value / size).

**`02_deep_portfolio_optimization.ipynb` — end-to-end MLP → softmax weights, trained on Sharpe loss**

- **The natural home for a book of alphas.** Each asset's input features become "my alpha signals on that asset"; the network learns the conditional combining rule, subject to sum-to-one softmax allocation, optimizing live-Sharpe rather than signal-IC. This is the modern successor to Markowitz for multi-signal shops.
- **Test-set Sharpe of 0.22 here is a warning, not a verdict.** Likely causes: 20 assets on 1 training year (2017), long-only softmax can't short, no regularisation. Fix by (a) training on 10+ years, (b) replacing `Softmax` with a `Tanh → normalize-to-L1` for long-short, (c) adding entropy regularisation on weights to prevent collapse.
- **Extension.** Add a **costs-aware** term to `Neg_Sharpe`: `loss = -mean(R) / std(R) + λ * mean(|Δw|)` with realistic bps cost `λ`. This alone usually flips OOS results.

### Chapter 7 — Limit order book

**`01_limit_order_books.ipynb` — LOB structure and visualisation**

- **Feature-engineering starter kit.** The 40-column LOB schema (Ask/Bid × Price/Volume × 10 levels) is the industry standard. Build a `lob_features.py` with: mid-price, micro-price (`(P_b * V_a + P_a * V_b) / (V_a + V_b)`), order-flow imbalance (L1 only and top-N), depth imbalance, weighted spread. These are the inputs every LOB model in the literature uses — they are not in `Utilis/` yet, and adding them is the obvious first contribution to this repo.
- **Execution / market-making.** Micro-price and OFI are directly actionable: short-horizon they predict next tick direction well enough to inform passive-vs-aggressive routing decisions and quote skew.

**`02_predictive_signal_lob.ipynb` — DeepLOB (inception-CNN + LSTM) for mid-price direction**

- **Most directly production-relevant notebook in the book.** DeepLOB is published, benchmarked (Zhang, Zohren, Roberts 2019), and the code here is a clean PyTorch port. If you have any LOB data — internal tick data, LOBSTER-format, exchange-colocated — this model is deployable as a short-horizon signal with perhaps a week of engineering.
- **Trade it three ways.** (1) As a signal inside a market-making agent — bias quote skew. (2) As a sweep-timer — wait for a predicted up-tick before lifting the offer. (3) As a pure directional stat-arb on the book's own prediction, sized by predicted magnitude.
- **Extensions worth doing.** Change the target from next-mid-return to **next-mid-direction** (three-class: up / flat / down with a dead-zone), which empirically trains better; add **volume-clock** rather than tick-clock sampling so you aren't over-weighting quiet periods; train separately for open/close/intraday regimes.

## Part 3 — Cross-cutting takeaways

Three patterns the book gets right that are worth internalising across your stack:

1. **Train on the utility, not on intermediate targets.** `SharpeLoss` and `Neg_Sharpe` in `Utilis/loss.py` are the single most portable idea in the book. Once you have them, the question "what should I forecast?" becomes "what weights maximise my actual P&L objective?" — a better question.
2. **Rolling-forward / walk-forward CV is non-negotiable.** The Ch. 4 harness is the minimum. Anything less (k-fold, random split) silently inflates Sharpe by 1–2x in OOS.
3. **Early stopping + state-dict checkpointing.** The `EarlyStopping` utility is small but the pattern — save state-dict only, load with `load_state_dict`, not pickled objects — is the right habit for every training run you ever do (model-class changes stay backwards-compatible with old checkpoints).
