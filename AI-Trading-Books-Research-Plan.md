**Working plan · 26 November 2026 · Eight part-time weeks at roughly 5–7 hours per week.** The time budget is a planning assumption. The two decisions are whether a learned momentum signal improves Korkea’s existing ETF trend sleeve, and whether a variance forecast improves the existing variance-risk-premium entry gate of the SPX short-volatility strategy. ## Start with the momentum benchmark

Korkea already has the expensive part of the first experiment in place. The local QP-118 configuration specifies the `etf-qp118-tsmom` universe, 252-session momentum, 60-session volatility, month-end rebalancing, a 10% annual portfolio volatility target and a 4× gross cap. It specifies 5 bps of slippage and $0.005 per share commission. Its Moonshot implementation observes the close on T, enters at the close on T+1 and first earns the T+1 to T+2 return. Freeze these conventions for the comparison. They are benchmark settings, not a proposed risk budget for deployment.

## Experiment 1 — Can a learned signal improve ETF trend?

**Comparison.** A is the exact QP-118 implementation. B is a regularised linear score using the book’s lagged return, trend sign and moving-average features. C is a small MLP using B’s inputs. Map B and C to the same bounded exposure convention before QP-118 sizing. Keep a simple fixed blend of the features available as a diagnostic if B and C both beat A. This distinguishes a gain from adding features from a gain attributable to nonlinear modelling.

**Sample and labels.** Freeze ETF eligibility as of each date, preserve inception dates and record whether the universe itself was selected retrospectively. Train across instruments but split all instruments on the same dates. Each training example should earn the forward return between achievable rebalance fills. A close-to-close label starting on the signal day credits the model with exposure it could not hold. Retain daily marked returns for measuring drawdown between rebalances.

**Training.** Use the local locked Python environment for PyTorch and scikit-learn. Fit scalers and hyperparameters inside the training window. For a portfolio Sharpe objective, aggregate concurrent held positions into one return per date before computing Sharpe. Use contiguous time blocks when charging turnover, include inherited positions at block boundaries and handle zero standard deviation explicitly. The book’s shuffled minibatches cannot measure chronological turnover. Use matched costs and sizing in training where feasible and the full implementation in evaluation. The book’s network takes 10 lags of 6 features into hidden layers of 64 and 32 units, about 6,000 parameters. A pooled monthly ETF panel may supply only a few thousand overlapping, cross-correlated examples, so fix a smaller architecture and its regularisation before training and do not search over it. If daily examples with month-ahead labels are used to enlarge the sample, purge labels that overlap a fold boundary. Record the book’s feature conventions when reproducing them: `ewm(k)` sets the centre of mass rather than the span, the moving-average differences are scaled by their own rolling standard deviation, and warm-up gaps are filled with zero. `Utilis/early_stopper.py` does not save a checkpoint on the first epoch, so a run that never improves reloads whatever file is already on disk.

**Promotion rule.** Specify tolerable drawdown and turnover before training. The decision statistic is the information ratio of the daily net active return (B−A and C−A), pooled across the out-of-sample development folds, with a block-bootstrap interval. Set the minimum acceptable value before training. Sample length governs what can be concluded: the standard error of an annualised Sharpe or information ratio is roughly 1/√years, about 0.7 over the two reserved years and about 0.3 over ten pooled out-of-sample years. The reserved sample therefore acts as a veto. A clearly negative active return there rejects the candidate, and a positive one does not promote it without the pooled development evidence. Check whether C adds anything over B. Re-run at twice assumed slippage and remove the largest contributing ETF. Report whether the advantage survives, its uncertainty, and any change in gross exposure. Prefer B if its net result and risk are comparable. Retain A if neither challenger earns the extra complexity.

## Experiment 2 — Can a variance forecast improve the SPX VRP entry gate?

**Baseline first.** Both implementations already gate entries on a variance risk premium, so the experiment changes one input of an existing rule. Backtest evidence comes from `vola_sell_index` in QuantConnect, which has the longer option history. Shadow observation runs in `vrp_qr` in QuantRocket, which already trades on paper. Restrict both to SPX. Snapshot the current `vola_sell_index` config and code. Use its actual entry clock, contract selection, exit policy, fees and existing gates, and leave every other overlay at its committed setting. Replay a normal entry, a blocked entry and an exit. Keep a missing mandatory VIX-curve input as a recorded block. If the baseline cannot be replayed like for like, finish the variance forecast study and defer the trading conclusion.

**Forecast test.** Fit with the history observable at the decision time. Aggregate predicted daily variances over the gate’s 30-calendar-day horizon and compare with the sum of subsequent squared index returns over the same sessions. Score positive forecasts with QLIKE (log forecast variance plus realised variance divided by forecast variance). Show scores in calm, rising-volatility and stressed periods. Candidates are trailing realised variance, EWMA, GARCH(1,1) refitted at each decision date (the `Chapter2/03` notebook fits once on the full sample), and a ridge regression on trailing realised-variance features. The ridge follows the structure of Hands-On §06/11, which predicts forward-week volatility for futures from trailing standard deviation, ATR and open interest. A Chronos forecast run locally can join on the same dates; it is optional and does not need the QuantRocket sidecar. Compare the winner with the gate’s current trailing RV30 on the same dates, since that is the input it would replace. A variance forecast that improves average error can still add nothing on the relatively few days when an option entry is eligible.

**One trading change.** Replace trailing RV30 in the VRP gate with the forecast annualised volatility over the next 30 calendar days. Keep the IV30 source, the threshold of 15 log points and every other gate, veto and overlay unchanged. Specify the substituted gate once, with its clocks and annualisation, and implement that specification in both `vola_sell_index` and `vrp_qr`; the port left several QuantConnect features out, so check that the two produce the same gate value on common dates. A substitution moves decisions in both directions: it can block entries the baseline took and admit entries the baseline blocked. State in advance what share of decisions you expect to change. If almost none change, the experiment ends with the forecast study.

**Decision.** Compare net P&L, worst cycle, drawdown, margin use, exposure days and counts of accepted, rejected and unavailable opportunities. Mark newly blocked baseline trades through their original exits, and simulate newly admitted trades under the baseline’s own exit rules. Compare against a control with equal average exposure, and inspect whether one crisis or one lucky skip accounts for the result. Bootstrap by cycle or contiguous blocks that respect overlapping positions. Use simple stress scenarios as a supplement when the sample contains few large losses. Start shadow observation only if downside improves without unacceptable foregone net return under the predeclared criteria. A tiny trade count calls for more observations. If entries are roughly monthly, “insufficient evidence” is the likely outcome for the trading leg within eight weeks. State beforehand what forecast-level result would justify continuing to shadow observation.

The Hands-On example estimates a two-state Markov regression and switches between long and short ATM straddles using long-dated contracts. Neither Korkea strategy needs its contract-chain code. A later, separate experiment can add a regime variable using probabilities available on the decision date and naming states by their estimated variance after each refit. Reconstructing historical decisions from probabilities smoothed on the full sample would introduce future information.
